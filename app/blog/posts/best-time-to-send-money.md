## How USD/PHP moves

The Philippine peso fluctuates against the US dollar based on several factors:

- **BSP monetary policy:** Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas interest rate decisions affect peso strength. Rate hikes strengthen the peso; cuts weaken it.
- **US Federal Reserve:** Fed rate changes move the dollar globally. Dollar-strengthening = fewer pesos per dollar.
- **Oil prices:** The Philippines is a net oil importer. High oil prices pressure the peso.
- **Remittance flows:** Heavy remittance months (December, June for school fees) can temporarily strengthen the peso due to dollar inflows.
- **Global risk sentiment:** In crisis periods, investors flee to the dollar — strengthening USD against all emerging market currencies including PHP.

## When NOT to send

### 1. Right after a Fed rate hike
When the US Federal Reserve raises rates, the dollar typically strengthens — meaning fewer pesos per dollar. If a hike just happened, wait a few days for the rate to settle.

### 2. During Philippine holidays
On Philippine holidays, banks are closed and the peso can't be traded normally. The rate you see might be stale and unfavorable. Wait for normal banking hours.

### 3. When the peso is artificially strong (December)
December sees the highest remittance volume of the year — OFW money for Christmas. This dollar flood can temporarily push the peso stronger (fewer pesos per dollar). If you can send in November or January instead, you might get a better rate.

### 4. Friday afternoons
Sending on Friday afternoon US time means the transfer won't process until Monday in the Philippines. You're locked into Friday's rate and it could move against you over the weekend.

## When TO send

### 1. When the rate is above the 30-day average
If today's rate is higher than the 30-day moving average, it's a good time to send. You don't need to catch the peak — you just need to be above average.

### 2. Mid-week (Tuesday–Thursday)
Exchange rate volatility tends to be lower mid-week. Monday has weekend gap effects; Friday has position-closing by traders. Tuesday through Thursday typically offers more stable, representative rates.

### 3. After peso weakness from external shocks
When the peso drops due to external events (oil prices, global market sell-offs, regional crises), USD buys more pesos. If the shock is temporary and unrelated to the Philippines' fundamentals, it's a good window to send.

### 4. Before heavy remittance months
If you know December will flood the market with dollars (weakening the dollar against the peso), send in November or early December before the peak inflow.
