Western Union and MoneyGram are the two oldest names in money transfers. Both have agent locations across the Philippines, both offer cash pickup, and both have been sending money to Filipino families for decades.

But legacy doesn't mean value. We compared them head-to-head on the USD → PHP corridor to see which one actually delivers more pesos.

## The short answer

Neither is the cheapest option for sending money to the Philippines. Wise and Remitly both beat them on price. But if you need cash pickup — especially in provincial areas where digital wallets aren't common — these two have the largest networks.

Between the two: **Western Union wins on network size and online pricing. MoneyGram wins on explicit fees at the counter — but loses on exchange rate margins.**

## Western Union: The strengths

[**Send with Western Union →**](/go/westernunion)

**Largest agent network.** Western Union has over 10,000 pickup locations across the Philippines — from Manila to Tawi-Tawi. If your recipient is in a remote province, WU probably has a location nearby.

**Online pricing is better than the counter.** The online service charges $0 fees (bank-funded) and uses a ~1.8% margin. That's not great, but it's far better than the cash counter, which charges $15+ and applies a ~3% margin.

**GCash delivery.** Western Union's online platform can deliver directly to GCash wallets — useful if your recipient has GCash but you want WU's network as backup.

**The problem:** That 1.8% online margin is the highest among digital-first providers. On a $500 transfer, you lose ~₱540 to the FX markup. On $1,000, it's over ₱1,000 — just for picking Western Union.

## MoneyGram: The strengths

[**Send with MoneyGram →**](/go/moneygram)

**Lower explicit fee.** MoneyGram charges $5 for a $500 transfer — lower than WU's $15 cash counter fee. But the FX margin (~1.5%) still makes the total cost higher than digital providers.

**More pickup locations.** MoneyGram partners with Palawan, Cebuana, and M Lhuillier — giving it roughly 15,000+ pickup locations. That's more than Western Union, especially in rural areas.

**Mobile wallet support.** MoneyGram can deliver to GCash and Maya wallets, though not as seamlessly as Remitly.

**The problem:** MoneyGram's online experience is clunky compared to Wise, Remitly, or WorldRemit. The app works, but the interface feels dated. And the exchange rate margin isn't competitive with digital-first providers.

## When to use each

### Use Western Union when:
- Your recipient is in a remote province with no bank or GCash access
- You need cash pickup at the most locations
- Your recipient prefers the familiarity of WU (many older Filipinos trust the brand)

[**Send with Western Union →**](/go/westernunion)

### Use MoneyGram when:
- There's a Palawan or Cebuana near your recipient but no WU
- You want a slightly lower explicit fee than WU's cash counter
- You're sending to a GCash/Maya wallet and don't have a Remitly account

### Use neither when:
- Your recipient has a bank account or GCash → use [Wise](/go/wise) or [Remitly](/go/remitly) instead
- Your recipient needs the most pesos possible → use [Wise](/go/wise)
- You're sending $1,000+ → the FX margin gap makes WU and MoneyGram significantly more expensive

## The verdict

**Western Union** has the brand recognition and the largest legacy network. **MoneyGram** has more pickup locations through local partners. But both are more expensive than digital-first alternatives.

If your recipient needs cash pickup in a specific location, check which provider has an agent nearby — that's the deciding factor. If both have agents, pick whichever has the better rate on the day you send.

For everything else: [Wise](/go/wise) for the best rate, [Remitly](/go/remitly) for speed, [WorldRemit](/go/worldremit) for cash pickup with better pricing.
